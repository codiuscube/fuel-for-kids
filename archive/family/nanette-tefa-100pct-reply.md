### Why 100% is the right default

- The equity statement lets NBCA "consider ... awarded funds" and "reduce, adjust, or revoke" aid when TEFA is available. Read plainly: aid is sized to the **award**, not to what we allocate. So any TEFA we park in Odyssey is money we'd have to replace out of pocket. The wallet would just be our own cash in a restricted account.
- **Rollover toward next year has little value under this policy.** Next year TEFA (~$31.4k) + NBCA aid is again capped at 90%; we pay 10% regardless. Rollover would only reduce NBCA's aid line, not ours. It only helps if NBCA cuts aid next year — and that insurance isn't free, because it costs the same cash this year.
- Odyssey accounts go inactive when a child graduates or leaves the program, and funds can't move between kids. Cassius is closest to that cliff.
- Applying 100% is also what the school clearly wants (it stretches their scholarship pool), and we care about aid continuity next year.
- The one scenario that flips this: if NBCA would top up aid so the family pays exactly 10% *regardless* of allocation. Then holding back is free money into the wallet. Nanette's conditional phrasing ("**if** you choose to apply 100%...") suggests that is *not* the case, but question 1 costs nothing to ask.

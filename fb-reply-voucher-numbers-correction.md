# Facebook Reply — Correcting the "Texas Voucher Update by the Numbers" Post

A few of these numbers are off. Sources at the bottom.

1. 118,441 is active AWARDS, not students funded. The Comptroller's Aug. 13 release puts funded participants at 101,600. At the Aug. 11 snapshot about 85,000 of the 118,441 were funded — families get four weeks to opt in and confirm before an award turns into money.

2. The 70% figure is the Tier 2 subgroup, not the program. Across all awardees, Comptroller data shows 43% previously attended public school. The rest is not all private/homeschool — 36,634 Pre-K and 25,909 kindergarten applicants had no prior schooling at all.

3. $6,215 is the basic allotment, a formula input, not per-student funding. Texas public schools spend about $13,189 per student in ADA (state and local) and about $18,972 per pupil across all funds. SB 2 sets the TEFA award at 85% of the statewide average state-and-local per-student amount — which is how you get $10,474. It is set below public per-student funding by statute.

4. Those two figures don't reconcile: 118,441 x $10,474 = $1.24 billion, more than the $1 billion appropriated. Not every award is $10,474 — homeschool/other awards are $2,000, and disability supplements run up to $30,000.

5. Crowell ISD has 58 applications in the Comptroller's own by-district spreadsheet. The report removes any district with fewer than 30, and says so in the file. Not being listed doesn't mean nobody applied.

6. Vouchers were on the agenda in two of the four 2023 special sessions, failed both times, and the program passed in the 2025 regular session.
